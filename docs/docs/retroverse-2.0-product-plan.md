## Working Definition

Retroverse is a time machine powered by popular music.

Every song is a doorway into the people, stories, culture, television, movies, events, and memories that made that moment matter.

Retroverse is not a playlist builder, DJ app, music database, trivia site, or card generator. Those are supporting systems.

The product is the experience of reconnecting with memory through music.

# What Is Retroverse?

Retroverse is a time machine powered by popular music.

Every song serves as a doorway into the people, stories, television shows, movies, events, trends, technology, and memories that defined a moment in time.

Retroverse is not primarily about music.

Retroverse is about remembering.

Music is simply the fastest way to get there.

A great Retroverse experience should make someone say:

"I remember that."

"I forgot about that."

"I was there."

"My parents used to play this."

"I haven't thought about this in years."

---

# Why Retroverse Exists

Most music platforms help people listen to songs.

Retroverse helps people reconnect with the world surrounding those songs.

The goal is not consumption.

The goal is connection.

When someone discovers a song in Retroverse, they should also discover:

* the culture
* the stories
* the people
* the events
* the memories

that made the song matter.

---

# The Core Promise

Retroverse helps people step back into the moments that shaped their lives.

The platform transforms songs into experiences.

A song is never presented as an isolated recording.

A song exists within a larger story.

Retroverse reveals that story.

---

# Who Retroverse Is For

## Primary Audience

People who have lived through multiple decades of popular culture.

People who enjoy:

* music
* memories
* stories
* television
* movies
* radio
* nostalgia
* collecting

These users are not looking for another streaming service.

They are looking for meaning, context, and connection.

---

## Secondary Audience

Music fans discovering earlier decades.

People who want to understand:

* why songs mattered
* why artists mattered
* why moments mattered

Retroverse gives younger generations a guided tour through cultural history.

---

## Event Audience

Patrons attending:

* Sunday Nights
* themed music events
* reunions
* festivals
* special presentations

For these users, Retroverse extends the live event beyond the stage.

---

# What Retroverse Is Not

Retroverse is not:

* a playlist builder
* a DJ application
* a streaming service
* a music encyclopedia
* a trivia game
* a card generator
* an AI experiment
* a database

These systems may exist inside Retroverse.

They are not the product.

---

# The Emotional Outcome

When a user leaves Retroverse, they should feel:

Connected.

Not informed.

Inspired.

Not educated.

Nostalgic.

Not researched.

The experience should feel like spending time with an old friend who remembers every great story.

---

# The Three Experiences

Retroverse exists in three forms.

## Live

A shared experience happening right now.

People gather around music and culture together.

Examples:

* Sunday Nights
* special events
* themed presentations

This is the heartbeat of Retroverse.

---

## Explore

Users follow songs, artists, years, and stories wherever curiosity leads.

This is the discovery experience.

---

## Collect

Users gather badges, passes, memories, favorites, and artifacts over time.

This is the personal experience.

---

# The Retroverse Test

Every future feature should pass this test:

Does this help someone reconnect with a memory, a story, a moment, or a piece of culture?

If the answer is no, it probably does not belong in Retroverse.

---

# The One-Sentence Definition

Retroverse is a time machine powered by popular music, helping people reconnect with the stories, culture, and memories behind the songs that shaped their lives.

---

# The Elevator Pitch

Imagine if every song opened a doorway into the year, the culture, the television shows, the movies, the artists, and the memories that made that moment matter.

That's Retroverse.

# Phase 2 — Patron Journey

---

## Guiding Principle

Every Retroverse experience begins at Live.

The user should never wonder:

* Where am I?
* What am I looking at?
* What should I do next?

---

## First Visit

Dave walks into The Main Pub.

A song is playing.

He scans a Retroverse badge.

He arrives at Retroverse Live.

---

## Retroverse Live

The first screen shows:

NOW PLAYING

* Song Title
* Artist
* Year
* Album Art

Large.

Readable.

Immediate.

---

## Why This Song Matters

Three short story cards.

Examples:

* Chart success
* Cultural moment
* Artist story

No deep reading required.

---

## Explore Further

Dave can choose:

* Song
* Artist
* Year

These are the primary paths.

---

## Search

Search is always visible.

Dave can search:

* songs
* artists
* years
* events

from anywhere.

---

## Exploring A Song

A song page should answer:

Why do people remember this song?

Not:

What is the metadata?

The story comes before the data.

---

## Exploring A Year

A year page should immediately show:

* biggest songs
* biggest artists
* biggest albums
* biggest movies
* biggest TV shows
* biggest events

The visuals tell the story.

The charts support the story.

---

## Returning Visitor

When Dave scans again:

Retroverse detects that a live event is active.

The Live experience remains the default destination.

The current event always comes first.

---

## Discovery Loop

Live
→ Song
→ Year
→ Related Songs
→ Related Artists
→ New Discovery

The journey never reaches a dead end.

# Phase 3 — The Retroverse Channel

Status: Draft

---

## Core Principle

Retroverse is always on.

When a live event is active, Retroverse follows the event.

When no live event is active, Retroverse follows a curated cultural journey.

The visitor experience remains the same.

Only the source changes.

---

## The Channel Model

Retroverse behaves more like a television channel than a traditional website.

Visitors arrive in the middle of an experience.

They do not begin with a menu.

They begin with something already happening.

---

## Live Mode

Triggered by VirtualDJ.

Displays:

* Current song
* Artist
* Year
* Story
* Explore options

Indicator:

LIVE NOW

Example:

Sunday Nights
The Main Pub

Currently Playing:
You Can Call Me Al
Paul Simon
1986

---

## Explore Mode

Activated when no live event exists.

Displays:

NOW EXPLORING

A featured song selected from a Retroverse journey.

Examples:

Summer of 1967

The MTV Years

One-Hit Wonders

This Week in 1978

Sunday Night Replay

Lost Classics

Road Trip Songs

---

## Programming

Retroverse can generate journeys of varying lengths.

Examples:

5 Songs
20 Songs
40 Songs
100 Songs

Each journey follows a theme.

The journey becomes the experience.

The songs are chapters.

---

## Navigation

The top navigation remains constant.

Retroverse

Search

Live

Years

Collections

The visitor should never feel like they entered a different application.

---

## The Homepage

There is no traditional homepage.

The Channel is the homepage.

The current experience is the homepage.

The current song is the homepage.

The current journey is the homepage.

---

## Long-Term Vision

A visitor can arrive at Retroverse at any time and discover:

* a song
* a story
* an artist
* a year
* a cultural moment

without needing to know what they are looking for beforehand.

## Phase 4 — Define Song States

Every song should fall into one clear state:

### Unknown

No RVTR. Show artist/title only.

### Identified

RVTR exists. Show basic song identity.

### Story Ready

Package exists. Show story, facts, culture, and context.

### Experience Ready

Deck exists. Show full swipeable Retroverse experience.

---

## Phase 5 — Visual Direction

Official Retroverse 2.0 style:

Blue Command Center visual system.

Tone:

* time machine
* broadcast booth
* night drive
* cultural channel
* live event companion

Avoid:

* museum
* database
* AI dashboard
* casino
* generic retro template

Design rules:

* large typography
* mobile-first
* simple navigation
* consistent cards
* clear hierarchy
* every page feels like the same product

---

## Phase 6 — Page Cleanup

Keep:

* Live
* RVTR song pages
* Deck viewer
* Year pages
* Command Center
* Package Review
* Creative Studio

Consolidate:

* song sheet
* deck page
* package page
* artist facts
* badge pages
* year tools

Retire:

* orphan utility pages
* duplicate admin pages
* experimental pages that do not support Live, Songs, Years, or Collections

---

## Phase 7 — 90-Day Plan

### First 30 Days

Define Retroverse 2.0 clearly. Simplify navigation. Make Live the front door.

### Days 31–60

Unify the song experience. One song page adapts based on song state.

### Days 61–90

Redesign Command Center and DJ Companion so Sunday Night can be run from one coherent place.

---

## Success Test

Retroverse 2.0 is working when:

A patron can scan a badge and instantly understand what Retroverse is.

A DJ can run a show without wondering where to look.

Every page feels like part of the same world.

The product feels alive, not archived.
